## v1.367 — Push-to-talk on the walkie, online, to your own side
Card `fable/d2-online-teams`, its second and last step: the push-to-talk half of backlog D.10 (Michael, 2 Oct: "a push to talk mechanism to compliment" the walkie, "a very fun and lighthearted feature for online play"; A on 3 Oct, push-to-talk waits for online; A on 21 Oct, "push-to-talk on the walkie for the people playing" with the teams). v1.366 put several people in one match; until now they could only hear each other if they were also on a phone call.

**What a player does.** In an online match with at least one other person on your side, a line under the squad order reads "📻 hold T to talk to your side" (R3, a click of the right stick held, on a pad). The first time you hold it the browser asks for the microphone; nothing asks before that, so a player who never uses it never sees the question. Once allowed, the mic is open but shut, and only goes live while the key is held: the walkie's talk-button click going down, "📻 TALKING TO YOUR SIDE", a lower click letting go. When a friend on your side talks you hear his click, then his voice through the walkie's sound, cut to the 450–2600 Hz band of a cheap radio with a little grit, and the line names him ("📻 Bluey"). The other side never hears you and never sees you key up. A new row in the settings, "Friends on the walkie (online)", on by default, mutes them and keeps your own walkie shut (the line then reads "Walkie muted · settings"). If the browser says no, the line says "No mic: the browser said no" and the match goes on. The kids' own walkie callouts are as they were.

**How it works.** The voice is WebRTC audio over PeerJS, the same library as the match's link, and goes straight from each person's browser to each of his side's people (a call one way per pair, so nobody gets a side's voice twice and no game has to mix sound). The host, who already knows everyone's link, tells each person at START which links are his side's (`mates`) and no others; a call from any other link is turned away unanswered. Who is keying up goes over the match's own link (`ptt`): a guest tells the host, who shows it if it is his side and passes it on to that side's other people only. The calls end with the match, and a friend who drops takes his calls with him; the mic itself is let go when you leave the room. Everything is behind `Net.active`, so the single-player game asks for nothing and loads nothing new. The one transport change is `voice(stream, links)` and two handlers, `voiceOk(link)` and `voiceIn(link, stream)`, so the tests' stub transport still stands in for PeerJS.
