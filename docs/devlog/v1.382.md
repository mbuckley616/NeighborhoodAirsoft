## v1.382 — Kids see your head over a wall, and shoot at it
Backlog B.20 (Michael, control room, 28 Oct): "There should be more of a tendency to try to issue suppressing fire on myself / enemies behind cover. I can simply stand and stare at enemies as they approach a wall I am behind, and wait for them to almost touch the wall before I shoot them." Especially in the Chattahoochee woods (the Hollow), and behind fort walls elsewhere.

**What was wrong.** Every sight test a kid made ran from his muzzle (1.05 m) to the target's *chest* (0.98 m on you), and nothing else. Stand behind anything taller than about a metre, a fort wall, a log pile, a car's hood, and your chest is hidden while your head and gun are over it in plain view. To the kid you were simply not there: he didn't stop to fight (that needs a line), didn't fire on the move (the marching and bounding suppression also needed a line to 1 m over your feet), and walked on at the wall until he came round it or close enough to see down onto you. Standing behind the wall drew exactly what crouching did. In a Seth 1v1 with a 1.25 m wall a metre in front of you, he walked to it and fired 0 times in 40 s standing or crouched (14 in the open); in the Hollow 3v3, 0 BBs at you in 40 s against 178 in the open.

**Now** (`js/80-ai.js`):
- `aiSightY(e, target)`: a kid sees the chest if it's clear, and if not, the head if *it* is clear (your eyes, standing or crouched; a kid's head box, lower when he crouches). Everything that asks "can he see him" uses it: the per-step sight memory, the commit to stop and fight while advancing, the bail-out from deploying, and the target scoring and team focus.
- When only the head shows, the shot is aimed at the head (the first BB and an auto burst's live follow-ups), not at the chest inside the wall.
- When he loses the line, his covering fire at where you were goes to where your head was, and so does the marching and bounding suppression, so a kid closing on a wall you were peeking over keeps rounds coming over its top instead of walking up silent.
- Crouched behind the same wall your eyes are below its top: nothing changes, you are hidden as before. A kid crouched behind cover is hidden from other kids the same way; one standing up to shoot over it can now be seen and shot at by your teammates (Michael's "enemies behind cover").

Nothing changes in the open: a clear chest is still the line and the aim.

### Still open
- How much fire a head over a wall should draw is a feel call: it now draws the same peek-and-shoot rhythm as being in the open, aimed at a smaller target, so many BBs hit the wall's top. A playtest in the Hollow forts will say whether it's too much.
- Coming uphill at a wall on a rise, a kid really can't see over it until he's close or off to the side (Seth's 1v1 from below: the head shows at about 9 m), so there he still closes before he fires. That's the geometry, not the old fault.
