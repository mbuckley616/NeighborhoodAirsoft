## v1.433 — The Derringer, a two-shot pocket pistol (gun batch 7, D.85); 42 guns
The second gun of batch 7 (Michael's **A** of 4 Nov: six more to 46, the kinds the builder's pick from what the shop is
thin on). Pistols were the thinnest kind at six, and none was a break-action, so this is the pocket Derringer, on the
mechanism v1.432 built for the Double-Barrel.

**The Derringer**, $35, under PISTOLS straight after the Spring Pistol: a tiny nickel-plated pocket pistol with a cream
grip and two short barrels (12 cm) one over the other, the kind that came in a cap-gun set.
- **Two barrels, a pull each.** The first pull fires the top barrel and it stays cocked; the second, once the shot's
  quarter-second lockout is out, fires the bottom one (`barrels: 2`, the Double-Barrel's rule in `onLmbDown`).
- **Then tip them up and load.** Hold LMB to tip the barrels up and drop two BBs in: 0.9 s (the Spring Pistol's slide
  0.7), about 1.05 s from the second shot to ready again. The cock bar reads BREAK OPEN · LOAD, and the hint "Click &
  hold LMB to tip the barrels up and load two BBs." (`updateLoadoutHud`; the Double-Barrel's still says shells).
- **The trade.** The short barrels throw the slowest pistol BB (27 m/s; the Spring Pistol 30), in the widest pistol
  cone (0.45; 0.40), and it starts to wander after 3 m (the Spring Pistol 4). It is the lightest gun in the game (1 lb;
  the Spring Pistol 2), so it has the fastest walk (1.18; every pistol and the MAC-10 1.15 at most). The tin holds 20
  BBs (a big tin 30 for $5; spare tins $2).

So it is a pocket gun: two quick shots while you run in close, then a second where you're loading it.

The first-person model is new: a nickel standing breech and frame, the barrel block on a hinge at the breech's top with
a rib and a front blade, orange at both muzzles, a hammer spur and a rear notch behind, a spur trigger with no guard,
and the Six-Shooter's bird's-head grip in cream, so the hand holds it as it holds the Six-Shooter. Loading it tips the
block up 33° on the hinge, the breech face swinging clear of the frame. It takes a sight and one rail (on the frame). A
kid holding one has a small nickel block on a cream grip, held as a pistol, but no kid carries one.
