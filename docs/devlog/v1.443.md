## v1.443 — The Foam-Noodle Cheek Pad (D.86, the third homemade wave's third part)
Michael chose **A** on "What the play lane builds next, after gun batch 7" (control room, 4 Nov): a third wave of homemade
parts, filed as backlog D.86. The cheek pad is the last of the three he named. Its effect was left to the builder.

**The Foam-Noodle Cheek Pad**, $5, 0.2 lb, in the STOCK slot of every long gun that has one (the 32; pistols and the
Uzi have none). It is 11 cm of green pool noodle, slit and pushed down over the top of the stock, with a band of duct tape
round noodle and stock at each end. A cheek pad puts your eye straight onto the sights, so **aiming in is 1.25 times as
quick**, and nothing else changes (the aimed cone is the bare gun's). Its trade is the slot. It takes the
STOCK slot, so it can't go on with the Sock-Padded Stock (aimed, a fifth tighter and a quicker walk). The Bungee Sling
(UNDER) aims in quicker still, 1.35, but costs a wider cone on the move. The pad and the sling stack, at 1.69.

**Where it sits.** It lies on the centre line, 1.5 cm short of the butt, with a third of its depth sunk over the comb.
The comb is the highest point of the gun along the noodle's length, not just the 5 cm butt box the sock is fitted to.
That box misses a stock that rises toward the grip. Each tape band reaches down to the bottom of whatever comes up near
the comb at its point, so a band wraps the stock and not the pistol grip below it. `measureGunForStock` now hands back
its boxes for this.

**Hidden once aimed.** In the first match picture, aimed on the AR, the noodle filled the middle of the screen: the
stock runs back under your eye, and a pad on it sits where your cheek is. So in first person its pieces hide from a
quarter aimed and come back below that (`updateGun`). The group stays visible, so the game still knows it is mounted.
At the hip it shows at the bottom right of the screen, past your hand.
