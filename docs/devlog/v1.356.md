## v1.356 — Music in matches: four loops by mode, and a sting on the result card
Backlog D.69 (Michael, control room, 23 Oct: "We need more music! Need some mode-specific or map specific tracks for in-game battles"). Until now only the bedroom had music; BEGIN stopped it and every match was silent but for the guns and the kids. His answer (control room, 27 Oct): **A, by mode: four loops plus win and lose stings**.

The loops are made the bedroom tune's way, oscillators scheduled ahead on the Web Audio clock, no files, and they reuse its bass, kick and chord-stab voices. Each is four bars of four beats in data (`MATCH_THEMES`, `js/26-audio.js`): the bars' chords and bass, which beats carry the kick, snare and stab, a 16-beat lead phrase and the cycles it plays on, so a loop breathes with a bar-long gap now and then.
- **Day** (team matches, 1v1s, free-for-alls, the race): 124 bpm in A minor, Am-F-C-G, a busy backbeat with eighth hats and a bright square lead three cycles in four.
- **Night**: 92 bpm in E minor, a kick on the one, soft hats, a long chord on beat three and a triangle lead every other cycle.
- **Tense** (every defend on a timer, the survive-untagged matches, and a Pickup Game or capture the flag on a clock): 140 bpm in D minor, four-on-the-floor kick, the bass pulsing eighths on the root and a lead that climbs bar by bar.
- **Finale** (each zone's last match on its ladder and the Last Saturday): 132 bpm, Am-F-G-Am, a stab on every beat and the lead up to C6.
The order is finale, then tense, then night, then day, so a zone's night finale plays the finale and a night defend plays the tense loop. A Pickup Game or online match never counts as a finale. The backyard range is practice, not a match, and stays quiet. Each loop has its own level, 0.075-0.09 against the bedroom's 0.18 (both under the Music slider), so a BB's crack and a kid's call carry over it.

At the result the loop stops and a sting plays: a win runs C-E-G up to a held C over a chord, a loss steps G-F-D down to a sagging C. A forfeit gets no sting. Going home brings the bedroom tune back, and M mutes the match loop and brings the same loop back. The match drums share one noise buffer (the bedroom's drums make a new one per hit, which at 140 bpm with eighth hats would be about 40 small allocations a second during play).
