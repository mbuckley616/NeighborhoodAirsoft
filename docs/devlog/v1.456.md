## v1.456 — The balance pass, step 4: a long still duel for the snipers (D.88)
v1.455's Still open: neither 1v1 in the table is the fight a bolt sniper is bought for (both kids walk up on you), so the
snipers could not be judged, and the L96 tuning tried there moved it only within luck. This version adds that fight to
`scripts/balance.mjs` and measures every gun in it. No game code changed.

**The duel** (`--maps duel_trey`). It is made in the page from Trey on Overwatch, for the measure only (the game never
sees it): Seth is taken out, and Trey alone, with his sniper as a defender, holds the front step across the street. The
bot stands on the street 45 m east of him (117.8, -1.0), where both lines are clear at every height by the BB's own
raycast. Three things had to be found first:
- From the match's own start (road_east, 57 m) a car and a tree hide him at every height: in the real match the player
  walks to a line. The bot is put on one instead.
- 50 m due east looked open to `hasLineOfSight`, but every one of Trey's BBs bounced back off the parked car 15 m in
  front of him (its cabin, 1.25 m up): his muzzle, lower than a standing player's eye, runs into it, and the clear-line
  check only casts a shot's first 3 m. He fired about 50 a round at a bot standing in the open and never hit it in
  150 s. The spot was moved 8 m north, where his line is clear too.
- Trey crouches behind his planter between peeks, so in the duel the bot shoots at his head when only his head shows,
  and it does not step out on first sight (it is in the open already; the 0.3 s step took it behind a car there).
The bot is otherwise v1.455's human bot. The worker's line now also counts the foe's shots (`foeShots`).

**All 50 guns, five rounds each** (250 rounds, 4 browsers, about 15 minutes; none reached the 150 s limit, the longest
took 26 s, 5 ended with no tag seen and count by the game's outcome, 4 losses and a win): the automatics own it. The
AK-47, Thompson, G36C, M4, RPK, M249, M60 and DMR won 5 of 5; the MP5, UMP, P90, Vector, AUG, Burst Rifle, M14, SVD, Ghost
Mini, Tick-Tock and Magnum 4. The Spring Sniper and the Pump Sniper won 3, the L96 2, level with the Lever Gun, the
Break-Barrel, the Bolt Pistol, the Auto Pistol and the Gas Carbine; the SPAS-12, Drum Shotgun, FAMAS, Electric Rifle,
Gas Pistol and Long Revolver 1. The other shotguns, the Spring Pistol, Derringer, Six-Shooter, Electric Pistol and
Snub-Nose, the Spring AR, the Clear Bullpup, the Rattle Can, the Clear Sprayer and the Bucket Gun won none: at 45 m they
don't reach or don't hold together, which is as they are meant to be.

The L96 is the outlier the table was built to find: the dearest bolt sniper loses the long duel to the cheapest, 7 of
20 against 16. Its rounds read the same way: a first BB that misses, then 1.1 s on the bolt while Trey's next shot comes;
13 of its 20 rounds were over by the bot's second shot. The Pump Sniper racks in 0.75 s and wins twice as often. The
automatics win every time by putting 12-13 BBs in the air while Trey works his own bolt, which no sniper can match at
45 m with this bot; whether a sniper should is the design's call, not the table's.
