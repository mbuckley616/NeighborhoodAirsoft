## v1.455 — The balance pass, step 3: the bot plays like a person (D.88)
Michael chose **A** on "D.88 balance pass: what the 1v1 table can tune" (control room, 6 Nov): make the bot human, then
measure again. v1.454's bot aimed perfectly and fired a quarter second after it saw him, so any gun whose first BB
landed won, and 40 of the 50 guns won 7-10 of 10 whatever they cost. No game code changed in this version: it is the
measure.

**What the bot does now** (`scripts/balance.mjs`; `--perfect` keeps v1.454's bot to compare):
- It holds the gun low until it sees someone, then aims in at the gun's own speed (a sling or a heavy gun is slower).
- On first sight it steps out to the side for 0.3 s, as a player comes out from behind whatever he saw him round. The
  walk shakes the gun at the game's own rate (`Game.player.sway`, so a foregrip or a sock stock tells).
- It reacts in 0.4-0.6 s (drawn each round), and again after he has been out of view for half a second.
- Its aim wanders round him: a smooth random walk that turns over in about 0.3 s, widest on the flick (0.03 rad, about
  60 cm at 20 m) and settling, the longer it holds still, to about a hand's width at 20 m (0.005 rad). The step out
  shakes it loose again. It follows a running kid a beat late (0.1 s).
- It taps a semi no faster than about six a second. Hold-over, racking, jams and reloads are as in v1.454.

**What it says:**
- **The two matches now test different things.** At the lot (Marcus with his pistol, first seen 40 m off) the bot won
  222 of 250: there is time to settle, and nearly everything wins. Against Sean (his AK, round a corner at about 13 m)
  it won 87 of 250: a reflex fight, and the half second a person takes now costs the round.
- **Against Sean the light, quick guns win and the long ones lose.** The Vector 5 of 5; the MP5, the P90, the MAC-10,
  the Bolt Pistol, the Snub-Nose and the Clear Bullpup 4. Every sniper and marksman rifle bar the Spring Sniper (1) lost
  all five (the Pump Sniper, the L96, the SVD, the DMR), and so did the M60, the Bucket Gun, the Rattle, the Thunder
  Pump, the Spring Assault Rifle and the FAMAS.
- **Price explains less than it did.** Across the 50, price against wins was 0.55 on v1.454's table and is 0.24 now
  (0.05 against Sean alone). That is not a flat table any more: what a gun buys now depends on the fight. Dear long
  guns lose a corner fight, cheap pistols win one.
- **The cheap end is where it should be**: the Rattle 4, the Derringer 4, the Tick-Tock 4, the Spring Pistol 5.
  But the Break-Barrel, v1.454's 37 of 40, is now 4 of 10, and the L96 ($190) is the bottom at 3.
