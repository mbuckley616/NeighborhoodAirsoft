## v1.458 — The balance pass, step 6: held automatics climb (D.88)
Michael chose **B** on "D.88 balance pass: what comes after the L96" (8 Nov): give held automatics a climb, then measure
again and tune from that table. Until now a held automatic stayed as steady as a tapped semi: nothing moved the aim
while the trigger was down.

**The climb** (`gunClimbKick`, `settleGunClimb`, `gunClimbMult` in 74-gun-hud; the camera in 72-player):
- Every shot of a full-auto gun kicks the aim up 0.003 rad (a heavy rifle at 600 a minute, from the hip, standing),
  0.7-1.3 times that each shot, and a little to one side at random. The first shot of a pull goes where you aimed, so a
  tapped automatic shoots as before; semis, racked guns and burst guns never climb.
- The aim comes back down on its own: slowly while the trigger stays held (a time constant of 1 s, so a long burst walks
  up and levels off), quickly once you let go (0.15 s). It never climbs more than 0.08 rad.
- How far it climbs: by the gun's weight class (light 1.6, medium 1.25, heavy 1, very heavy 0.7) and its rate (a shot's
  kick goes as the root of 600 over its rate, so a fast gun climbs faster but not in step with its rate); aimed a fifth
  less, crouched 0.85; the **Sock-Padded Stock** 0.7 and the **Shoelace Sling** 0.75 (their shop lines say so now); the
  rubber-band bipod's legs take off up to four-fifths, as far as they are down.
- It sits on top of where you point (`Game.gun._climb`, `_climbYaw`), added to the camera's pitch and yaw: you pull down
  against it, and your saved aim never moves. The gun in your hands goes up with the camera.

Held for 2 s from the hip, the AK climbs 0.026 rad (about 50 cm at 20 m), the MAC-10 0.059, the M60 0.018; the AK aimed
0.020, aimed with the sock stock and the shoelace sling 0.010.

**The table again.** `scripts/balance.mjs`'s human bot now pulls down against the climb as a person does, a beat late:
it sees the climb 0.15 s after it starts and takes back 80 % of it (the perfect bot takes back all of it). `--noclimb`
switches the climb off to compare; `--climb 2` tries it twice as strong, for the measure only.

And the 22 automatics and two controls in all three matches with the climb, 5 rounds each (the AK 10 of 15, the M4 11,
the Vector 12, the M249 11, the M60 11; the Pump Sniper 10 and the M14 10 as controls), against v1.455-v1.456's tables
for the same guns without it: within luck everywhere. Against Sean the light automatics still win the corner fight,
at the lot nearly everything still wins.

**What it says:** the climb is not what wins the duel for the automatics. The tags come 1.6-2 s after first sight,
12-17 shots in, and most of those shots go out in the first second, before the climb has built: the bot pulls the rest
back down. Even three times as strong it takes one or two rounds in 20 off them. What decides the long duel is the
volume an automatic puts in the air while Trey works his bolt. So the climb is kept at the size above, for the feel
(a long burst walks off; a short one doesn't), and nothing was tuned off this table. What comes next is a question in
decisions, "D.88 balance pass: after the climb".
