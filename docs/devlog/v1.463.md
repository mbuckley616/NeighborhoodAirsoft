## v1.463 — Recorded voices, step 3: the packing script, and the recording evening
Card `fable/d9-recorded-voices`, backlog D.9, its last step. v1.461 named every line and wrote the script, v1.462 plays
a clip when there is one; this is how a folder of VoiceStudio's clips gets into the game, and what Michael and a local
session do on the evening they record.

**`node scripts/voicepack.mjs <folder>`.** Every file in the folder is named for its clip, as docs/voice_script.md says
(`seth.taunt.3.wav`, `seth.teammate.4.mp3`). It checks every name against the script and turns away, with the reason,
a name the script hasn't got, a kid who isn't in it, another kid's line under a name (`seth.trey.taunt.1`), a shared
line from a band the kid doesn't draw from, a `{name}` line without its `@name`, a second file for one clip, and a clip
shorter than 0.2 s or longer than 8 s. It reports how many of the kids' own lines are recorded and how many are still
missing, kid by kid (`--missing` lists them, with the words), and any clip in the pack whose line has since changed. Then it
writes `js/28-voice-clips.js`: each clip with the line's words, its length and its audio. The folder is the whole pack
(`--keep` adds to what is there instead; `--dry-run` writes nothing; `--clear` empties it). No packages: it reads a WAV's,
an Ogg's (Opus or Vorbis) and an MP3's length itself. **A WAV becomes an MP3** when ffmpeg is installed: mono, 24 kHz,
40 kbps, with the silence before and after the words cut off (VoiceStudio's exports pad both ends). MP3 because every
browser decodes it; an Ogg won't on an older Safari (it would fall back to the browser voice, v1.462).

**What a minute of audio costs**, in the file (base64 is a third larger than the audio), measured here:

| Export | MB a minute | The 752 own lines (about 16 min) |
|---|---|---|
| MP3, mono, 40 kbps (what the script makes of a WAV) | 0.40 | about 6.5 MB |
| MP3, mono, 48 kbps | 0.51 | about 8 MB |
| Ogg Opus, mono, 32 kbps | 0.34 | about 5.5 MB (but not on older Safari) |
| WAV, 24 kHz 16-bit (no ffmpeg) | 3.8 | about 60 MB: refused |

The 16 minutes is 752 lines at about 1.3 s each (they are eight words or fewer, mostly three to five). **Proposed cap:
8 MB** for `js/28-voice-clips.js` (`--max-mb` to change it): every kid's own lines at 40 kbps fit with room, which is
the part of the pack that matters most (a kid says his own line 7 times in 10), and 8 MB is a few seconds' download
on itch's page once, then cached; the browser reads it as text at startup and decodes nothing until a line is said.
The shared lines would be up to 33 readings each of 201 lines (about 4,600 clips, 40 MB): too many for one page, so the
script recommends leaving them to the browser voice, or recording only a few kids' (the crew) if the mix of a real
voice and the browser's grates. Whether to raise the cap is Michael's call once he hears a pack.

### Still open
- **For Michael, with a local session (an evening; the session runs the commands):**
  1. **Install VoiceStudio** from https://voicestudio.sh/ (the source is https://github.com/debpalash/VoiceStudio): the
     local session follows its README for your computer, and installs ffmpeg beside it (`brew install ffmpeg` on a Mac,
     `winget install ffmpeg` on Windows) so the clips come out small.
  2. **Pick a voice per kid.** Open `docs/voice_script.md`: each kid has a one-line brief (age, how he talks, height,
     how pushy). Fix the briefs first where they are wrong (they are guesses from the code; you know the real kids:
     change `VOICE_BRIEFS` in `scripts/voicescript.mjs` and run it). In VoiceStudio make or choose one voice for each
     kid and note it down, so a re-record sounds the same; two kids should never share a voice (the twins Haden and
     Connor least of all, since they shout for each other).
  3. **Record a kid at a time, his own lines first**, reading each line in the tone the script gives. The session can
     cut the kid's table out of the script into VoiceStudio's batch input if it has one.
  4. **Export names:** each clip saved as its id from the script plus `.wav` (or `.mp3`), all in one folder, say
     `voices/`: `seth.taunt.3.wav`. A shared line is the kid's id, a dot, the line's id: `seth.teammate.4.wav`. Skip the
     `{name}` lines.
  5. **Pack it:** `node scripts/voicepack.mjs voices` (`--dry-run` first to see the report). Fix what it turns away, run
     it again, then `node scripts/parsecheck.mjs` and `node tests/run.mjs voice-clips`.
  6. **Listen:** play a match on Seth's court with the session's build open in your browser; the kids you recorded talk
     in their own voices, the rest in the browser's. Loudness (`VOICE_CLIP_GAIN` in js/20-characters.js, 0.6) is the
     first thing to tune.
  7. **Ship it** on a branch with a PR labelled `hold`, so it waits for your Approve: the clips are your choice of voices.
     The `voices/` folder stays out of the repo (the repo is public, and the clips are already in the pack).
